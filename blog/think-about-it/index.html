When a prospect says "I need to think about it"

If someone has been thinking about a problem for two years, three more days is not the issue. Here is what is really going on and what to ask instead.

By Chris Deloach · October 9, 2026

You get to the end of a good conversation and you hear it. "This all sounds great. I just need a few days to think about it."

Most owners say "of course" and book a follow-up. Then they chase that person for a month.

Here is the thing I want you to notice. If someone has been living with a problem for two years, they have been thinking about it for two years. Three more days is not going to produce anything new.

It is not a time problem. It is a problem problem.

"I need to think about it" almost never means they need time. It means one of two things. Either the problem you talked about is not the real one, or it is real and they do not yet feel what it is costing them.

Both of those are on us, not on them. Calling people tire kickers is victim thinking. If it keeps happening, the odds say it is the process, not an endless supply of bad prospects.

Pressure-test the problem early

You find out in the first few minutes whether there is anything to solve. If they cannot answer your first three questions clearly, there is no defined problem yet, and no amount of follow-up will create one.

So ask early, and ask plainly:

  • What made you book this call now?
  • How long has this been going on?
  • What have you already tried?

Then ask the one question that matters

What happens if you do not fix this?

Say it and go quiet. If the honest answer is "nothing," then nothing is exactly what they are going to do, and you just saved both of you a month. If the answer has weight to it, you now have a real conversation.

What to say when you still hear it

Do not argue and do not chase. Get curious. "That is fair. When you think about it over the next few days, what specifically will you be weighing?" Whatever they say next is the real objection. Now you can deal with it today, while they are in front of you.

The goal of a sales conversation is not the close. It is clarity. When you both have it, the decision is easy, whichever way it goes.
